Origins (Devon, England, 15th century)

The earliest documented Coffins lived at Portledge, Alwington, Devon (Devonshire), and at Butler’s Parish, Brixton, South Hams. The line is documented from around 1425.

Generation outline (early generations from source PDF pp. 7–8)

15th century — founders

  • Richard Coffin (b. 1425, Portledge, Devon) m. Alice Gambon (b. 1425, Merston, Shropshire) in 1456.
  • John Coffin (b. 1450, Portledge)

16th century

  • Sir James Coffin/Coffyn & Mary Ann Cole — parents of Peter Tristram (below)
  • Peter Tristram Coffin (b. 1535, Portledge, Devon) m. Mary Boscawen (b. 20 Jan 1542, Cornwall) in 1555. Died 16 Nov 1601, Portledge.

Late 16th century

  • Nicholas Coffin/Coffyn (Nov 1561, Brixton – 5 Oct 1613, Brixton) m. Joan Avent in 1577. Six children incl. Peter, Tristram, Nicholas.

17th century — Mayflower era

  • Peter Coffin (Coffyn) (b. Jan 1580, Brixton) — son of Nicholas
  • Tristram Coffin (b. 11 Mar 1609, Butler’s Parish, Brixton) — emigrated to America, became a founder of Nantucket
  • Dionis Stevens (b. 4 Mar 1610, Brixton, Devon) — wife of Tristram

Tristram Coffin and Dionis Stevens are central early-American figures; their descendants spread through New England and eventually Wisconsin and Michigan.

The line that connects to the Snoeks (19th century, Maine → Wisconsin)

  • Stephen Coffin & Elisabeth Weston — parents of James R Coffin (b. 17 Jun 1784, Freeport, Cumberland, ME)
  • James R Coffin (1784 – Feb 1860, Leeds, Androscoggin, ME) — children include Samuel, Nathan, Reuben, George Washington, Elizabeth
  • Samuel Coffin (1785–1786, infant death) — or a later Samuel; the source has multiple Samuels
  • Samuel Coffin m. Harriette Smith on 20 May 1837, Isle au Haut, Knox, ME. Six children:
    • Eliza, David, Martha Ann, Matilda, Clara Barbara, Phoebe
  • Phoebe Coffin (1847–1935) m. (1) Peter Harder (1830–1879), then m. (2) John Orren Snoek (1817–1890) — the union that joins the Coffin line into the Snoek family.

Mayflower / Pilgrim context

The source document devotes significant space (pp. 8–11) to the Pilgrim migration to Holland and the Mayflower voyage. Tristram Coffin (b. 1609) was born just one year after the first Pilgrim migration to the Netherlands. While he is not a Mayflower passenger himself, he is part of the same generation and emigrated to Massachusetts a few decades later.
